# T7: render speed without a GPU

October 2026, 4 vCPU cloud machine, no GPU, chrome-headless-shell 1243 (software compositing), 1920x1080 30 fps.

## Where the time goes

`node bench/speed/trace.mjs examples/data-story/index.html` (a Chrome trace around 45 frames of one worker) gives, per
frame (`bench/speed/layers.mjs` lists the composited layers and why they exist):

| work | share |
|---|---|
| `SoftwareRenderer::DoDrawQuad` (the display compositor drawing layers, one thread per page) | ~60% |
| raster of layer content (`RasterTask`, worker threads) | ~25% |
| our JS (`EvaluateScript`: seek, timeline, hooks) | ~3% |
| JPEG encode + transfer | small |

So the cost is compositing: how many layers there are and how much of the frame they cover. Data-story composites about
7 frames' worth of layer area per frame (three blurred background blobs, a grid with a mask, two scene layers, the root).
The benchmark page, which renders 3x faster per worker, has fewer big layers.

## What was tried (each config twice; the machine was shared, so ±3-5 s noise on ~25 s renders)

| change | result |
|---|---|
| workers 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 on 4 cores | 4 (= cores, the default) is as good as anything; 2 is clearly slower |
| `--num-raster-threads=1` | no change |
| `--quality draft` (JPEG 80, x264 ultrafast) | no clear change (encode is not the bottleneck) |
| `--disable-partial-raster` | no measurable cost; now the default because it fixes a determinism bug (below) |

## What changed

- **Encoder stall at part boundaries (fixed).** The new fixed 1-2 s parts (T4) waited for each part's ffmpeg to finish
  before capturing the next part (10 s of "encoder flush" in a 53 s profile). Parts now finish encoding in the background
  while the next part is captured.
- **Determinism (fixed).** Chrome's partial raster redraws only the changed part of a tile on top of pixels from whenever
  the tile was last drawn, so a page with a line drawing on in a zoomed world gave one frame that differed by one level
  in four pixels between 1 and 3 workers, on the old engine too. `--disable-partial-raster` fixes it at no measured cost;
  `test/features.test.js` has the reproducing page.

## Round 2: layers (task/speed-layers)

The machine was shared with other agents (load average 7-21 on 4 cores at times), so wall time was too noisy to use.
The main metrics are deterministic or nearly so: composited layers and their area per frame (from LayerTree, clipped to
the frame), render passes per frame (counted in a trace), and the CPU *thread time* (`tdur`) of
`SoftwareRenderer::DoDrawQuad` (the compositor draw), which is much less sensitive to other load than wall time.
`bench/speed/trace.mjs` now prints thread time and event counts.

### What a layer costs (synthetic pages, 1920x1080, compositor draw CPU per frame)

| page | draw ms/frame |
|---|---|
| solid background only (solid-color tiles are nearly free) | 7-9 |
| + full-frame picture layer, whole-pixel position | +3 |
| + the same, at a fractional position (bilinear filtering) | +5 more |
| + full-frame `<canvas>` at full resolution | ~0 |
| + full-frame `<canvas>` 240x135 scaled up (gradient-bg's approach) | +7.5 |
| + `filter` on a full-frame layer (even `blur(0px)`: an offscreen pass, then a filter) | **+20** |
| + `isolation: isolate` on a full-frame scene | 0 |
| + a layer with `opacity: 0` | 0 (not drawn) |

So the expensive things are offscreen passes (filters, also *potential* filter animations), scaled or fractionally
positioned layers, and full-frame layers in general. Layer *area* alone is only a rough proxy, which is why the report
also counts passes.

### What changed

1. **`givemotion bench` prints a compositing report** (`layerReport` in `src/bench.js`, also
   `node bench/speed/layers.mjs file.html [t...]`): average layers, area (frames of pixels) and render passes per frame,
   the same per sample time, and the biggest layers with their element and reason. Layers with opacity 0 are skipped.
2. **Idle enter/exit animations no longer hold layers** (`seekAnimations` in `src/runtime/runtime.js`). Chrome keeps an
   element on a composited layer while any animation on it is "about to play" or in effect, and paused animations
   count. So every scene with a transition kept its exit animation's layer, and for blur/whip/zoom exits an offscreen
   filter pass, *for the whole scene*. Our own animations (enter, exit, camera, travel, morph) are now cancelled while
   they have no effect (exit before it starts, enter after it ends; setting `currentTime` brings them back), and while
   one holds its first keyframe before it starts (fill backwards) it gets a playback rate of -1: same look, but not
   "about to play".
3. **Finished CSS animations that end on the element's own style drop their forwards fill** (`trimFill` in
   `src/runtime/runtime.js`). `animation: fade-up .6s both` (the documented pattern, and most blocks) kept the element
   on a layer for the rest of the clip, because the fill keeps the animation in effect. When the `@keyframes` rule sets
   none of the animated properties at `100%`/`to` (all presets except draw/wipe/spin...), the end state *is* the
   element's own style, even if that style changes later, so `both` → `backwards` (`forwards` → `none`) changes no
   pixel. Only for `direction: normal`, whole iteration counts, `composite: replace`.
4. **Still blur blobs bake to an `<img>`, moving ones keep the `<canvas>`** (`optimizeBlurs` in `src/runtime/motion.js`).
   A canvas is always a layer, and so becomes everything drawn on top of it. A blob that does not move (no CSS/WAAPI
   animation, timeline tween, path travel or camera on it) is now painted once into the layer around it. For moving
   blobs an image was measured too: draw -3 ms but raster +2..8 ms (the blob is re-rastered as it moves), so they keep
   the canvas.

Tried and dropped: an opaque canvas (`alpha:false`) in gradient-bg (22.4 vs 20.4 ms, no gain); gradient-bg's grain
moved under the canvas with the canvas at `1 - grain` opacity (same blend, one layer less: 21.2 vs 20.4 ms, within
noise); removing `.scene { isolation: isolate }` (20.7 vs 20.9 ms on vertical-short: no cost, and blocks rely on it).
Blocks (gradient-bg, grid-bg) are unchanged: what they cost is their look (see the ablation below).

Block ablation (test page with one block, draw ms/frame, 3 runs): empty scene 8.8; gradient-bg 20.5 (without grain
18.4); grid-bg 26.3 (without vignette 24.0, without glow 21.4, without the drifting grid 14.7).

### Results

Layer report, 12 samples over the video (deterministic; most frames take 2 passes even with no filter on screen):

| example | layers before → after | area (frames) before → after | passes before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| data-story | 7 → 7 | 4.99 → 4.99 | 2.6 → 2.1 |
| product-promo | 7.7 → 6.9 | 4.52 → 4.14 | 3.3 → 2.9 |
| vertical-short | 5.9 → 5.4 | 5.18 → 4.68 | 2.8 → 2.5 |
| bench page (`examples/bench`) | 8.6 → 4.8 | 1.86 → 1.80 | 3.2 → 2.3 |
| data-story with still blobs (drift removed) | 7 → 4 | 4.62 → 3.35 | 2.6 → 2.1 |

Compositor draw CPU per frame (`DoDrawQuad` thread time, one worker, 90 frames, 3 interleaved runs each, median).
Measured twice, once with load 1-2 (changes 2 and 4) and once with load 20-38 (all changes); the thread times agreed
within ~1 ms and runs within ±2 ms:

| example (window) | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| data-story (4-7 s) | 45.4 ms | 36.2 ms (-20%) |
| product-promo (4.5-7.5 s) | 36.2 ms | 31.6 ms (-13%) |
| vertical-short (4.2-7.2 s) | 36.9 ms | 26.5 ms (-28%) |
| bench page, checklist scene (7-10 s) | 7.7 ms | 8.0 ms (passes 4.9 → 2.3, but small layers: no gain) |
| data-story with still blobs (5-8 s) | 47.1 ms | 31.0 ms (-34%) |

Raster thread time was unchanged within noise (data-story 34.2 → 34.7, product-promo 6.0 → 6.0, vertical-short
19.1 → 21.0). Full renders (default 4 workers, 3 interleaved runs each, load 7-21, changes 2 and 4 only): data-story
25.5 → 25.9 s, product-promo 30.4 → 30.1 s, vertical-short 27.0 → 31.2 s (medians); single runs of the same config
differed by up to ±25%, so this says nothing either way. It needs a quiet machine.

Frames (full videos, PNG, before vs after, all changes): data-story 423 of 462 identical, the rest at most 1 level in
at most 0.02% of the pixels (a thin gradient line, dithering). vertical-short 209 of 330 identical, the rest almost
all 1 level (up to 30% of the pixels in one scene, where text and shapes that were their own layer now paint into the
layer below), a few pixels at 8-9 levels on the edge of a dot. product-promo 553 of 663 identical, the rest 1-7 levels,
except the accent line under the title during the slide transition: its antialiased edge lands a fraction of a pixel
differently (up to 43 levels on 40 edge pixels). Bench page: 1 level, except that a dimmed bar of the bar chart now sits
on the baseline where the old render left a 1 px dark gap (the layout puts it on the baseline; the gap was the old
layer's pixel snapping). The still-blob page: max 1 level in 6% of the pixels. Looked at the frames and zoomed,
level-stretched crops: no visible difference except the gap, which is now correct. 1 worker == 4 workers pixel for pixel
on vertical-short, the bench page and the still-blob page; `test/features.test.js` has a test for changes 2-4
(fails on the old engine).

## Ideas not done

- Compare with a GPU machine (`--gl gpu`), and with 8-16 cores, where more workers should scale.
- Wall time on a quiet machine for the changes above.
- Most frames take 2 render passes even without a filter on screen (a frame of the bench page with 13 layers takes 1);
  find out what the second one is.
- The remaining big costs are in the background blocks and are part of their look: gradient-bg's scaled canvas
  (~7.5 ms) plus the grain layer, grid-bg's drifting grid (fractional position, ~6-12 ms with the vignette pulled
  into a layer above it). Ideas that change pixels slightly: grid drift in whole pixels, vignette as a mask on the grid.
- data-story's raster (~55 ms/frame in the chart scene: an SVG line with a `drop-shadow` filter redrawn as it grows).
